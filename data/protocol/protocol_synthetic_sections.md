## 1. Study Overview [SYNTHETIC]

Paritty Study LS-2026-01 is a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multi-site Phase 2 study of a transdermal patch in subjects with mild-to-moderate Alzheimer's disease. The treatment period is 24 weeks, followed by a final follow-up visit at Week 26. Approximately 400 subjects are enrolled across 24 sites. This protocol summary is synthetic: it was written for a software demonstration and is modeled on the structure of the public CDISC pilot study. It is not a real clinical protocol and must not be used for any clinical decision.

Subjects are randomized at Baseline in a blinded manner. Treatment assignment is held by the unblinded statistician only and is never shown to sites, monitors or data managers.

## 4. Additional Screening Requirements [SYNTHETIC]

Written informed consent must be obtained before any study procedure is performed. Subjects must be between 50 and 90 years of age at consent. Seated systolic blood pressure at Screening 1 must be below 160 mmHg. Alanine aminotransferase (ALT) at Screening 1 must not exceed 1.5 times the upper limit of normal (ULN). Total bilirubin at Screening 1 must not exceed 1.5 times ULN. Creatinine clearance must be at least 30 mL/min.

Subjects who fail screening may be rescreened once, at least 4 weeks after the original screening date, with sponsor approval. A subject must have a caregiver who can attend visits and supervise daily patch application. Questions about whether a specific caregiver is reliable, or whether a specific medical condition makes a subject unsuitable, are medical judgments and must be escalated to the medical monitor.

## 5. Visit Schedule and Windows [SYNTHETIC]

Each scheduled visit has a window of plus or minus 7 days around its planned study day. Screening 1 occurs at Day -7. Baseline occurs at Day 1 and is the day of randomization. Week 2 is planned for Day 14, Week 4 for Day 28, Week 6 for Day 42, and Week 8 for Day 56. Week 12 is planned for Day 84, Week 16 for Day 112, Week 20 for Day 140, and Week 24 for Day 168.

Week 26 is planned for Day 182 and is the final follow-up visit. Visits at Week 10, 14, 18 and 22 are telephone contacts. Vital signs are measured supine for 5 minutes, then standing for 1 minute and 3 minutes, at every clinic visit. Hepatic and hematology laboratory tests are drawn at every clinic visit and sent to the central laboratory.

## 6. Dosing and Study Treatment [SYNTHETIC]

Study treatment is a transdermal patch applied once daily and rotated between application sites. The dose is blinded, and sites must never record or discuss the treatment assignment. If a patch is missed, apply a new patch as soon as possible but never apply two patches on the same day. Study treatment stops on the date of discontinuation and must not be dispensed afterwards.

Application site reactions such as redness, itching or irritation are expected adverse events and are recorded with their severity. A kit of patches is dispensed at each clinic visit from Baseline onwards, and the kit number is recorded in the interactive response system.

## 7. Prohibited and Restricted Medications [SYNTHETIC]

Anticholinesterase inhibitors and memantine are prohibited during the study. Strong CYP2D6 inhibitors are prohibited. Topical corticosteroids at the patch application site are restricted and require medical monitor approval. Paracetamol up to 2 g per day is permitted for pain or fever.

All concomitant medications taken from 30 days before Screening 1 until the end of the study are recorded with start and stop dates. The protocol does not list herbal or dietary supplements; whether a specific supplement is acceptable is a medical judgment and must be escalated to the medical monitor.

## 8. Safety Monitoring and Liver Stopping Rules [SYNTHETIC]

Hepatic laboratory tests (ALT, AST, alkaline phosphatase and total bilirubin) are measured at every clinic visit. A subject with ALT or AST at or above 3 times ULN and total bilirubin at or above 2 times ULN must be reported to the medical monitor within 24 hours. Repeat the liver tests within 48 to 72 hours of any value at or above 3 times ULN.

Study treatment must be interrupted if ALT or AST is at or above 5 times ULN. Study treatment must be permanently discontinued if ALT or AST is above 8 times ULN. These rules describe a potential liver safety pattern that requires medical review; they do not by themselves establish a cause.

## 9. Serious Adverse Event Reporting [SYNTHETIC]

All serious adverse events must be reported to the sponsor safety team within 24 hours of site awareness. A death must be reported immediately by telephone. The investigator assesses causality as related when there is a reasonable possibility that the study treatment caused the event. Follow-up information must be submitted as a new version of the case, and the safety database and the clinical database are reconciled continuously.

Sites must never unblind a subject on their own. Requests to unblind are emergencies that go through the medical monitor.

## 10. Data Management and Query Handling [SYNTHETIC]

Case report form data must be entered within 5 days of the visit. Queries must be answered within 10 days of being sent. Central laboratory results are transferred daily and reconciled against the EDC. Database lock occurs 5 days after the last open query is closed and all safety data are reconciled.

Every generated query, correction and review note is approved by a human before it is sent or takes effect.

## 11. Discontinuation and Withdrawal [SYNTHETIC]

A subject who discontinues must attend a final lab visit within 14 days where possible. Reasons for discontinuation include adverse event, withdrawal by subject, lack of efficacy, protocol violation and lost to follow-up. Withdrawal of consent does not remove data already collected. Study treatment must not be dispensed after the discontinuation date.
